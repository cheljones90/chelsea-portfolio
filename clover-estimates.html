Clover Estimates

Overview

Clover's professional services merchants were losing business to competitors. Without a native way to send estimates, they were forced to stitch together third-party tools like QuickBooks and HoneyBook for the first half of every job, then return to Clover to collect payment. The workflow was fragmented, the experience was broken, and Clover was losing merchants to platforms that owned the full loop.

I was brought in as sole design lead to build Estimates from the ground up — conducting research, designing every merchant and customer-facing touchpoint, and collaborating with Clover's engineering and product teams through to launch. The MVP shipped in October 2025. Post-MVP followed in February 2026, and the feature continues to evolve with advanced payment terms in active development.

The problem.

Contractors, consultants, and tradespeople don't just take payments. They scope work, send estimates for client approval, collect deposits, then invoice for the remainder. Clover had invoicing. It had payments. But the front half of that workflow — the estimate — didn't exist natively.

That gap forced merchants to use a separate tool, manually re-enter data when converting to an invoice, and present clients with documents from two different platforms. Competitors like Square and HoneyBook were winning merchants specifically because they owned this loop. Clover needed to close it.

Key Signal

No competitor owns the full estimate-to-payment loop natively within a POS ecosystem. That is Clover's advantage, and the foundation this feature was built on.

The approach.

Before designing anything, I mapped the competitive landscape across five platforms — QuickBooks, Xero, FreshBooks, Square, and HoneyBook — and interviewed six Clover merchants across the three core professional services personas: hourly, project-based, and milestone-based. The research pointed to a clear MVP focus: estimate creation, client communication, and invoice conversion.

From there I worked closely with Clover's product and engineering teams through PRD reviews, design critiques, and prototyping cycles — testing with merchants at each stage before handoff. I also collaborated directly with the Invoicing team to make sure estimates and invoices felt cohesive end-to-end, not like two separate features that happened to be adjacent.

Designed for both sides.

Estimates aren't just a merchant tool — they're a client-facing experience. The email and web views of an estimate were designed to feel professional and trustworthy, matching the quality of the payment experience clients already saw from Clover. OTP authentication on the approval flow gave merchants a formal record of client sign-off, removing the ambiguity of email and phone approvals.

The client-facing OTP verification flow (left) and web approval view (right) — designed for clarity and confidence.

A decision worth noting.

When the team wanted to give merchants a way to add project-specific information to estimates — like SKUs, work order numbers, or hardware codes — the obvious path was to repurpose Clover's existing Custom Fields feature. The problem was that feature was built to collect information from customers, used in hosted checkout and virtual terminal. Bending it to serve a completely different purpose would have broken a pattern merchants already relied on and created confusion about when to use which version.

I proposed a net-new feature called Reference Details — scoped specifically to estimates, with a single clear purpose: providing information to the client, not collecting it. A cleaner mental model, no risk to existing behaviour, and merchants can search their records by those reference fields. The proposal was approved and is currently in merchant testing.
